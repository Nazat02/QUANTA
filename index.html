Three hands, three measurements

Every observation returns three eigenvalues. Each has its own colour, reach and character.

Hours

violet-blue · shortest spike

Points to the hour on a twelve-hour dial and moves the least. Its tip is labelled H with the 24-hour value.

Minutes

cyan · middle spike

Sits between the other two and drifts smoothly as the seconds pass. Its tip is labelled M.

Seconds

ice-white · longest spike

The fastest hand, and the reading most likely to be wrong by the time you look again. Its tip is labelled S.

Four ways to see it

Particle
A bounded cloud of probability points that drifts, then collapses to three spikes. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom.
Gaussian
The same odds as a smooth density surface. Bell curves overlap before resolving to sharp peaks.
3D
An orbital shell around a glowing nucleus, with the spikes passing through it in full perspective.
Slit
Send the time through a double slit and watch it interfere with itself.

The quantum lab

Uncertainty principle
Hours, minutes and seconds don't commute. Measure one sharply and the other two blur.
Entanglement
Link two tabs, or pair another device. Observe one and the other collapses, each showing its own local time.
Schrödinger's alarm
Set a time and a chance it rings. Afterwards it is both ringing and silent until you observe it.
Quantum Zeno effect
Keep observing while a reading is on screen and it freezes in place.

The details

Observe

Press the Observe button or the Space bar. Repeat while a reading is showing to freeze it.

Explore

Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom from 0.35× to 7×. Press 0 to reset the view.

Colourways

Blue, red, green and yellow. Blue is the signature.

Adaptive density

The particle count adjusts so the cloud stays smooth on slower devices.

Remembers

Lab settings are saved in your browser and nowhere else.

Pairing

Device pairing uses WebRTC with a public STUN server. Codes are exchanged by hand.

Design notes, QUANTA

Questions

Is this a real quantum system?

No. Quanta is a visual thought experiment: your device's ordinary clock, presented with quantum vocabulary. The measurement is your system time, read at the moment you look.

Is the time really hidden?

The time is always your system clock. What changes is whether Quanta shows it. It appears when you observe and fades as your knowledge decays.

Do I need an account?

No. It runs in your browser.

How does device pairing work?

One device creates a code, the other joins with it and sends a reply back. It uses WebRTC with a public STUN server, and you exchange the codes by hand.

The time is unknown until you decide to know it.
